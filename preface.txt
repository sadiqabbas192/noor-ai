Sulaym ibn Qays Al-Hilaali al-Aamiri al-Kufi Tabe’ee al-Kabeer Abu Sadiq, *Qaddasa Roohahu* (May He make his soul pure) is from one of the children of Hilaal ibn Amir, who lived in Hijaz and was from the descendent of Ismaeel ibn Ibrahim (peace be upon them). He was born two years before the migration of the Holy Prophet (s.a.w.a.). At the time of the demise of the Holy Prophet (s.a.w.a.) he was twelve years old but he was not able the see the Prophet during his lifetime. He came to Medina at the time of his prime youth in 16 A.H., and he passed away in 76 A.H. He is called Kufi because he was born near Kufa, and after that he came to Kufa from Medina with Ameerul Momineen (a.s.) and made Kufa his place of residence. He also enjoyed the honour of being one of the companions of the five infallible Imams (a.s.) (i.e. Hazrat Ali, Hazrat Hasan, Hazrat Husain, Hazrat Ali Ibnul Husain Zaynul Aabideen, and Hazrat Muhammad ibn Ali al-Baqir [peace be on all of them]) and everyone trusted him and found him reliable. He was with Ameerul Momineen (a.s.) ever since he came to Kufa from Medina, and was from his special friends and those who knew his secrets – not all of his companions enjoyed that honour as is observed in Tradition No. 7 where he had requested that Ali (a.s.) prays for him that May Allah make him from his special friends (Wali) in this world and the Hereafter, so Ali (a.s.) said, ‘O Allah make him from my special friends’ and taught him the prayer which the Holy Prophet (s.a.w.a.) had taught to Salmaan, Abu Zar and Miqdaad which begins from Allahumma Ba-a’snee… ‘O Allah! Raise me in such a way that I believed in you, accepted your Messenger and accepted Wilayat of Ali (a.s.) and believed in Imamat of the
Imams from Ala-e-Muhammad (a.s.)’. He was with Ameerul Momineen (a.s.) in Battles of Jamal, Siffeen and Naherwaan, not only that but he was included in Shooraatul Khamees (from his special army) in which, companions like Maalik-e-Ashtar were included. He did not separate from Ali (a.s.) until the last moments of Ali’s (a.s.) life, and was present when Ali (a.s.) was making his wasiyyat. He narrated around fifty traditions of the Holy Prophet (s.a.w.a.), which he himself heard from Ali (a.s.) and used to confirm with him other traditions that some companions used to narrate. Among his specialities was that Ali (a.s.) had informed him of the events and discord that were to come like those mentioned in Hadees No. 67. Sheikh Abdul Husain Ahmed Ameeni Najafi, in his book Al-Ghadeer, Volume 1, page 195 writes in the margin: ‘The Book of Sulaym is from the famous books, and was very much in circulation in the old days and was accepted by the U’lama, narrators of traditions and historians of both Shia and Sunni faiths.’ Ibn Nadeem, in his book al-Fehrist writes on page 275 that Sulaym was from the companions of Ameerul Momineen (a.s.) and on page 307, he writes: ‘When the time of Sulaym’s death was very near, he said to Abaan, “I have a right over you, and I am going to die soon. O the son of my brother! What events took place during the time of the Holy Prophet (s.a.w.a.)!” and he gave the book to Abaan i.e. the book of Sulaym ibn Qays Hilaali, which is famous.’ And ibn Nadeem writes: ‘The first book that reached Shias was the book of Sulaym.’ Mas’oodi, in his book At-Tanbi wal Ashraaf, writes on page 198: ‘The origination of surety in the Imams of Isna Ashari is that which Sulaym ibn Qays Al-Hilaali has mentioned in his book.’
Imam Sabqi in Mahasinur Rasaael fee Maarefatil Awaael, writes, ‘The first book that was written in Shias was the book of Sulaym.’ Sheikh Amin (r.a.) writes: The alphabet LAM (ل) in the ‘Li Shia’ of ibn Nadeem is for benefit of Shia, which means that those people (Shias) declared it as an evidence and the opponents surrendered as the result of the evidence because they trusted Sulaym and accepted what he wrote as truth. They did not just accept what is written in the Shia book as enough but also accepted its authenticity. It is also observed from what Mas’oodi has written that he also has given the connection of Imamiah Sect who believe in twelve Imams to this Book because at the time when the discussion between Sunni and Shia was at its peak, it was not enough to just agree but what was discussed was accepted and believed, and it is for this reason that it has been referred to Sulaym, and many highly learned U’lama of the Sunni sect, like al-Hakeem al-Haskaani in Shaawaahedut Tanzeel Le Qawaaedal Tafzeel, Imam Hamwini in Faraaedus Simtayn, Sayyid ibn Shahab al-Hamadaani in Mawaddatul Qurbaa, Sheikh Sulaymaan Kanduzi in Yanaabeeul Mawaddah who are very well known and popular, have narrated traditions from him. After the above matters, it is clear that this book is such that both sects have accepted it. Of course there are many good comments made by others but I feel what is mentioned above is sufficient. Readers can refer to Al-Ghadeer, volume 1 page 195. Hazrat Ali (a.s.) informed him of some important events and disturbances (fitnah) that were to take place as observed from Hadees No. 67 onwards. ### Sulaym’s Birth \nIt is not definitely known when Sulaym was born but it is possible that he was born two years before Hijra as can be observed from Hadees No. 34 when Abaan asked Sulaym as to how old he was at the time of the Battle of Siffeen, and Sulaym replied that he was forty years old. Laylatul Hareer took place on  Safar 38 A.H. and this was the ending period of this war, and he was 40 years then. It is not known where he was born. It is also not mentioned in any books where he grew up. However, it can be certainly said that while the Holy Prophet (s.a.w.a.) was alive Sulaym had not visited Medina, and this is also supported by the fact that he does not narrate any Hadees from the Holy Prophet (s.a.w.a.), and also he has written Hadees concerning the events at Saqifa after having spoken to Ameerul Momineen (a.s.), Salmaan, Miqdaad, Abu Zar, Ibn Abbas, and Baraa ibn Aazib. So it is possible that he was not present then, and the assumption is correct that he started visiting Medina in 14 A.H.. The other evidence is this that Salmaan Farsi went to Madayan as Governor in 16 A.H. and did not return but Sulaym has mentioned a number of traditions from Salmaan. So, during these two years Sulaym benefited from Salmaan Farsi. \n### Enlightening period of Sulaym’s life \nNot many people are fortunate to be a writer as well as a warrior at the same time, as the thinking of both professions is very different. The combination of both professions can only be a great gift from Allah. If one studies deeply his life, it can be clearly observed that he was an extremely upper class warrior on one hand and an equally excellent writer who has, purely for the pleasure of Allah, collected
such a treasure of knowledge for the forthcoming generations that no matter how much the ‘people of pen’ feel proud, it is not enough. Anyone studying his life in detail can easily come to the conclusion that not only did Sulaym not like being famous or popular by writing the traditions, he hated such publicity since his aim was that the true matters reach people. If it was not for his hatred for his own publicity and popularity, the oppressors of those times, i.e. Governors, rulers and the enemies of truth, would have burnt all the books of knowledge that the generations have inherited from Sulaym and to support my conclusion the evidence I would like to present is the letter that Muawiyah wrote to Ziyad concerning the treatment that should be given to anyone who was a Shia of Ali (a.s.) and his Ahlul Bayt. No one would have known what Muawiyah had written, but Sulaym, through his wisdom, obtained this letter from the writer of Ziyad by befriending him. He took great pains to carry out in depth research and thinking, as is quite clear that he did not think it was sufficient to hear the matter from just one person. He did not feel satisfied until he had got any matter verified by very reliable and trustworthy people. His mentioning, ‘I have books that I have written in my own writing after having heard them from extremely reliable and trustworthy people. There is not a single tradition that I have heard from just one person. I have asked about the same matter to another person until a number of people confirm the same matter as being true’, greatly supports the conclusion that I have arrived at. In Hadees No. 24 he writes, ‘I heard from Salmaan, Abu Zar and Miqdaad and then asked Ali ibn Abi Talib (a.s.). ### His death \nAround the beginning of 76 A.H. he passed away and got the opportunity of being near the infallible personalities (a.s.). He became ill and died in the house of Abaan ibn Abi Ayyaash in Nawbandjan. Hajjaaj ibn Yusuf came to Kufa in 75 A.H. as governor of Kufa and as soon as he came, he started looking for Sulaym as is evident from what Abaan says i.e. ‘When Hajjaaj came to Kufa he asked about Sulaym. ### Sulaym’s Wasiyyat (Will) \nAbaan was the only person with the lone traveller whom he thought he could trust with his secret. When he became ill, he had first thought of burning the book but then thinking it to be a sin, he did not burn it. \nAt the time of his death he said to Abaan: ‘O son of my brother, I have spent time with you and I did not find anything in you that I did not like, and you have a right over me.’ And he also said: ‘You know what events took place after the passing away of the Holy Prophet (s.a.w.a.)?’ Then he told Abaan what was written in the book and the truth and authenticity of its contents, although most people will not accept it. \nHe also said to Abaan: ‘I have such matters that I have heard from very reliable people and have written in my own writing. It contains such traditions that I do not wish to disclose it to ordinary people, because they will reject them and will say it is impossible for such events to take place, and will not take them seriously. Of course this is all true, which I have heard from people who are truthful, jurists, honest and good people – i.e. Ali ibn Abi Talib (a.s.), Salmaan Farsi, Abu Zar Ghaffaari, and Miqdaad ibn Aswad. There is not a single tradition that I have accepted which I have heard from one person only, but I have asked other people also and when they all agreed then I wrote it down. There are some matters that I have heard from the truthful ones (infallible Imams [a.s.]) and then written them.’ \nThen he took a promise from Abaan concerning the book, and said: ‘Promise me, for the Sake of Allah, that until I am alive you will not talk to anyone about this, and after my death you will only tell them
